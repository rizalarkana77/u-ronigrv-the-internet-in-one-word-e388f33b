## The entry

**Slop**

One word for the whole internet, and nothing else.

## Why this word wins

The brief asks for one word that is funny, fits the entire internet, and is surprising — and "Slop" is the only candidate that nails all three at once in 2026.

- **It fits everything, not just one corner.** "Doomscroll" only describes how you consume; "cookies" only describes the annoyance; "enshittification" only describes the platforms. "Slop" describes what the internet *is* now, end to end: the feeds, the search results, the comment sections, the video thumbnails, the product reviews — an endless ladle of machine-churned, engagement-farmed, barely-edible content served at scale. Every surface of the modern internet is slop now, whether a bot made it or a human farmed it for the algorithm.
- **It is funny because it is an insult the internet earned.** Nobody calls a cathedral slop. The word carries the pig-trough image — content poured out by the bucket for creatures expected not to chew. It is rude, vivid, and instantly understood, which is exactly what a one-word joke needs: the laugh lands before the explanation starts.
- **It is surprising because it is current.** The expected answers (doomscroll, chaos, unhinged) describe the old internet's mood. "Slop" is the word the internet itself coined for its new reality — AI slop, content slop, slop feeds — so it reads as both a punchline and a diagnosis. Against any single-mood or single-feature word, it covers more of the internet in fewer letters, and its bluntness makes it the funniest honest summary available.

Four letters. No notes. The whole internet, served warm.

## How to run

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This writes the single word `Slop` (no trailing newline) to `out/entry.txt`, reads it back to verify, and prints its SHA-256 hash. The program is deterministic: every run writes identical bytes.
